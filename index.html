Australia's bushfire is often thought of as a mere natural disaster, but it is a climate emergency. As the temperatures rise, the fires burn more frequently, intensely, and release more carbon which creates a negative feedback loop making each season worse than before.

The Black Summer of 2019-2020 scorched more than 18 million hectares, which in comparison is more area than many countries. Thirty people died and nearly three billion native animals were killed or displaced. This visualisation explores the data behind Australia's escalating bushfire crisis, from the vast scale of destruction to the climate forces that drive it.

1. The Big Picture

Between 2012 and 2025, Australia recorded an upward trend in both the annual number of wildfire and the total area burned, indicating worsening fire seasons over time. Note that these totals include ALL fire types across Australia, which includes the Northern Savanna Burn, hence why annual figures reach 60 million hectares. The Black Summer's 18.6 million hectares refers to catastrophic forest and Bushfires in southern eastern Australia.

2. Where it Burns

Bushfires do not strike Australia evenly. Some states bear a disproportionate share of the destruction, year after year.

3. The Climate Link

Australia is getting hotter. As temperatures rise above the 1961–1990 baseline, the conditions for catastrophic fire become more frequent and more severe.

4. The Cost

Beyond the flames, the consequences of Australia's bushfire crisis ripple through ecosystems, communities, and the atmosphere itself.
