Australia is Burning

The story behind a decade of escalating bushfire seasons

Fire has always shaped the Australian landscape, but the fires we face now are different. Since the early 2000s, bushfire seasons have been arriving earlier than expected, lasting longer and burning with an intensity not experienced before. The Black Summer of 2019-2020 scorched more than 18 million hectares, which in comparision is more area than many countries. Thirty people died and nearly three billion native animals were killed or displaced. This visualisation explores the data behind Australia's escalating bushfire crisis, from the vast scale of destruction to the climate forces that drive it.

1. The Big Picture

Between 2012 and 2025, Australia recorded an upward trend in both the annual number of wildfire and the total area burned, indicating worsening fire seasons over time.

2. Where it Burns

Bushfires do not strike Australia evenly. Some states bear a disproportionate share of the destruction, year after year. (Charts coming soon)

3. The Climate Link

Australia is getting hotter. As temperatures rise above the 1961–1990 baseline, the conditions for catastrophic fire become more frequent and more severe.

4. The Cost

Beyond the flames, the consequences of Australia's bushfire crisis ripple through ecosystems, communities, and the atmosphere itself.
